Generative AI Is Two Shifts, Not One
When people talk about generative AI, they typically think of one major shift. In reality, there are two distinct
transformations happening, and most organizations are only addressing the ﬁrst.
The First Shift: AI as a Helpful Tool
The ﬁrst shift came with ChatGPT and similar tools. Static software became conversational AI for individuals.
People can now get instant answers, draft documents, and explore ideas through natural conversation. This is
valuable, but it's fundamentally still a tool that humans use—you ask, it answers.
The Second Shift: AI as a Coworker
The second shift is more profound. AI moves from answering questions to taking autonomous action. These are
AI agents—digital colleagues that can carry out knowledge work on your behalf. An agent doesn't wait for you
to ask a question. It monitors your inbox, processes documents, coordinates tasks, and proactively reaches out
when something needs attention.
This second shift is where the signiﬁcant value lies. An AI agent can work 24/7, handle scale that humans
cannot, and free your team to focus on work that requires human judgment and creativity.
Abundly is fully focused on this second shift—on AI agents. We help organizations build human+AI teams
where agents and people work side by side as colleagues.
Why Most Agent Initiatives Fail
Everyone talks about building and deploying agents. What few acknowledge is that most agents end up sitting
unused. The pattern is remarkably consistent:
1. IT purchases an AI agent platform
2. Someone builds an agent
3. The people who should use it don't understand how it works
4. It becomes a black box—nobody can iterate or improve it
5. The agent is never quite good enough (and agents are rarely perfect on the ﬁrst try)
6. It sits unused, and the investment is wasted
This is the black box problem. The knowledge of how the agent works sits with whoever built it—often an
external vendor or a single technical person. The domain experts who actually need to use and improve the
agent are locked out.
And here's the critical insight: agents need continuous iteration. Workﬂows change. Edge cases appear. Users
have feedback. An agent that can't be improved is an agent that will eventually fail.
The black box problem is why most agent initiatives fail. The solution isn't better technology or more features.
The solution is ensuring the right people can build and iterate on their own agents.
